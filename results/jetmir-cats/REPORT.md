# Jetmir, Nexus of Revels — how this deck works
*Each creature card in the 99 must be a Cat, changelings excluded, and each creature token created under my control must be a Cat.*  ·  87 cards + 12 basics + commander

## How it wins
The deck wins by attacking with a wide board of Cats while the commander is out. Three creatures gives the team +1/+0 and vigilance, six adds another +1/+0 and trample, nine adds a third +1/+0 and double strike. A 1/1 Cat token then connects for eight, so eight attackers deal 64 - enough for two opponents on turn seven, when a life total that began at 40 has drifted toward 24. First backup: Jazal Goldmane, whose ability gives attacking creatures +X/+X for X attackers, turning a board of six into a kill without the third tier. Second backup: Biorhythm, which sets each player's life total to their creature count - eight mana, but it sidesteps blockers and damage prevention, which the primary plan cannot.

## The line
Turns one to three: noncreature ramp (Sol Ring, Mind Stone, the green land-fetchers) plus cheap Cats - Ocelot Pride, Sacred Cat, Wild Nacatl. Turn four: the commander, with Snakeskin Veil held up. Turn five: a multiplier - Arahbo, the First Fang, Esika's Chariot or Felidar Retreat. Turn six: push toward nine with Regal Caracal, Leonin Warleader or Second Harvest. Turn seven is the commit turn - answer the permanent that blanks the attack, cross nine, swing. Turn eight follows up, where Nacatl War-Pride pays for itself: one tapped attacking copy per creature the defender controls.

## What the deck is made of

**body count** (21 cards, answers W1). Twenty-one slots: the third tier reads nine creatures, and the pool holds 121 Cat creature cards against 2,087 noncreature cards. The criterion is bodies per card, not stats per card. Answers W1.

**commander insurance** (4 cards, answers W3). Four slots at one or two mana. The commander has no entry trigger and no self-protection, and a tax of 4 / 6 / 8 / 10 makes a third cast a lost turn. Answers W3.

**damage conversion** (7 cards, answers W4). Seven slots to close link six: eight attackers at the third tier deal 64, killing two opponents out of three. Answers W4.

**flex** (2 cards, answers W1). Two slots held from the plan turn, spent on an instant-speed token doubler and a Treasure engine that pays the commander tax. Answers W1 and W5.

**interaction** (7 cards, answers W4). Seven slots aimed at permanents that blank an attack - taxers, large blockers, hosers - not at threats in general. Instant speed. Answers W4.

**lands nonbasic** (22 cards, answers W5). Twenty-two slots. The criterion cost the deck its fetchlands, which produce no mana of their own on the turn played; I kept it rather than widening it after seeing the cards. Answers W5.

**ramp** (8 cards, answers W5). Eight slots, noncreature by force - a mana creature outside the Cat subtype is not legal. The pool replaces the function whole. Answers W5.

**refuel** (5 cards, answers W6). Five slots: creature-count-scaling draw is four cards in the pool, so two of those plus three generic pieces that work from an empty board. Answers W6.

**token engines** (6 cards, answers W1). Six slots, the hardest to fill: just 23 pool cards make a Cat token. The rest rests on the plan-turn finding that a copy of a Cat is a Cat, which widens it to doublers. Answers W1 and W2.

**wipe insurance** (5 cards, answers W2). Five slots, sized against 2.8 live answers on the commit turn - one saving effect is under-built. Instant speed and board-wide by criterion. Answers W2.

## Against the table
What beats this deck is repeated sweepers plus a taxing enchantment: each sweeper costs several cards at once, and a tax charging per attacker charges eight times over. Hold Heroic Intervention or Make a Stand rather than deploying into open mana, and save Krosan Grip for the enchantment. A board of small Cats looks harmless, and until the third tier switches on this reads as the weakest deck at the table - trade on that. It stops looking harmless when the sixth creature lands and trample appears.

## Piloting it
Mulligan: keep two lands with two plays before turn three, or three lands with one multiplier; ship one-landers. Count bodies, not power - cast the cheap Cat first. Tokens arriving tapped and attacking raise the count immediately, so Leonin Warleader and Nacatl War-Pride can cross a threshold inside their own combat. Skullclamp works against the plan while the board climbs; hold it until past nine or after a sweeper. The destroy-and-compensate answers hand a token to the controller of what they destroy, so point them across the table - a token from one would be a non-Cat body and would break the restriction in play. The commander has no targeted ability; the land decision is what the green tutors fetch - a Forest-typed dual such as Canopy Vista over a basic when white or red is short. Finally, Kaheera, the Orphanguard meets its companion condition automatically here; moving her outside the 99 gains a card at the cost of this slot.

## Known weak slots
Mirror March, Nacatl War-Pride, Biorhythm, Skullclamp. These are the first cards to swap when the collection grows.
